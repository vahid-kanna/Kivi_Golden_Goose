# Part One: Product Position and Vision

## 1. Product Positioning Statement

For software builders, founders and mainly the cross-functional operators who work across the multiple tools, Kivi is a voice-first computing layer that turns daily spoken dictation into the durable personal context. Unlike conventional dictation tools that discard your words the moment they are pasted into an app, Kivi runs quietly across your workflow, remembers the people, commitments and numbers you speak out loud and gives you an assistant in Hey Kivi that can recall and connect past work with exact source proof without demanding any manual organization.

---

## 2. Product Vision Document

Most of the voice software treats dictation like as an alternative to the mechanical keyboard. You press a key, speak a sentence, text drops into an active window and the audio disappears. The software does not know if you just scheduled a deployment, promised client a date, or filed a site report. It only cares about audio precision and comma placement.

The real opportunity for Kivi is different. People who build and run things do not think in clean documents, they think out loud across twenty different browser tabs, Slack threads, terminal windows and email drafts. In ordinary work, they constantly speak high value context that never gets written down anywhere permanent. They will never take thirty minutes at the end of the day to update a personal knowledge base or maintain notes in a separate app.

Semantic memory should turn Kivi into a quiet personal operating layer. It should sit underneath everyday work and build context purely as a byproduct of regular dictation. When you dictate an update to a colleague, Kivi does not just drop text into the chat box. It notes down that Priya is reviewing the backend migration next week, that the site holding cost was calculated at thirty four thousand rupees a day or that the beta launch date is set for October 21.

A chatbot that talks back while you dictate would be annoying. Normal dictation must stay completely without disruption. The real leverage appears when you say Hey Kivi to connect dots across different tools. When you walk into a meeting and ask, "What did I tell the team about the database refactor yesterday?" Kivi pulls the exact facts from your scattered Slack and email dictations.

For this to work, the boundary of what Kivi remembers must be strict. Kivi should keep hard invariants: names of people and their responsibilities, concrete numbers and metrics, explicit milestone dates and the formatting style you naturally prefer in specific apps. It should deliberately ignore background noise, filler language and incomplete thoughts.

More importantly, Kivi must never assume unstated intent. If an engineer dictates that the server response time is doubled during a demo, Kivi records the operational fact. It must never jump to the conclusion that the release failed or that the contractor underperformed. It must never assume an aspirational comment like "I hope we wrap this up by Friday" into a firm deadline and then if someone asks Hey Kivi about an event or decision that was never recorded, it must simply say it does not know rather than making up an answer from generic training data.

People will only keep Kivi on their laptops if they feel completely safe with it and this requires two things one is the localfirst storage where raw speech and memory graphs never leave the machine and the second is complete source tracking. Every time Hey Kivi answers a question, it must show the receipt having the exact application, timestamp and sentence where the information came from.

Kivi shouldn't be an annoying executive coach or just another high maintenance notetaking app. It should simply be the most reliable and truthful memory of what you have actually worked on.
